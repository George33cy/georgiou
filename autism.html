Overview

Project description

The DIACNOSE project investigates speech characteristics in adult native speakers of Cypriot Greek with autism and neurotypical controls. It combines speech analysis, statistical modelling and machine-learning methods to examine whether patterns in speech can support the study and assessment of autism and related communication or neurological disorders.

Results

Main findings and implications

The project showed that autism is associated with measurable and systematic differences in adult speech, but not with a single uniform autistic voice. Autistic speakers displayed a more expanded and dispersed vowel space, while other differences were selective, vowel-specific and multidimensional, with HNR emerging as one of the more consistent group-level markers.

Machine-learning models further demonstrated that speech patterns contain meaningful information for distinguishing autistic from neurotypical speech, reaching an AUC of about 0.89 under token-level validation. However, performance fell to around 0.60 when tested on previously unseen speakers, showing that individual speaker characteristics strongly affect generalisation.

The key implication is that speech has strong potential as an objective, non-invasive source of information for autism research and future assessment-support tools, but reliable clinical application will require larger samples, cross-speaker validation and language-sensitive models rather than assuming a universal acoustic profile of autism.
