Research

My research aims to advance understanding of speech, language, and communication by integrating interdisciplinary perspectives and methods from linguistics, artificial intelligence, psychology, clinical neurology, and related fields. It focuses on real-world challenges, including improving outcomes in nonnative language learning and supporting the assessment, identification, and monitoring of neurological disorders.

Theme 1

Speech Perception and Production

Motivation: Speech perception and production are central to spoken communication, language learning, and social interaction. In educational contexts, especially in second-language learning, pronunciation, listening comprehension, and spoken interaction can strongly affect learning outcomes. This creates a need to understand how people perceive, categorize, and produce speech sounds, and why these abilities vary across individuals and contexts.

Methodology: My work combines behavioral experiments, acoustic analysis, perceptual testing, and computational modelling, including machine-learning approaches, to examine speech perception and production. A central part of this work is the Universal Perceptual Model, which I developed to predict patterns of nonnative speech acquisition based on acoustic and perceptual similarity, with the goal of improving learning outcomes and explaining individual differences.

Keywords: Speech Perception, Speech Production, Second-Language Learning, Universal Perceptual Model, Acoustic Analysis, Machine Learning

Theme 2

Speech and Language Biomarkers of Neurological Disorders

Motivation: Neurological disorders can affect speech and language in subtle ways that are not always captured through traditional assessment. Changes in voice quality, speech timing, phonological processing, grammar, and language use may reveal important information about cognitive and neurological functioning. This creates a need for objective speech and language biomarkers that can support earlier detection, assessment, and monitoring.

Methodology: To address these challenges, my research integrates methods from acoustic analysis, linguistic profiling, perceptual measurement, machine learning, and explainable AI. I examine how speech and language features distinguish typical from atypical communication patterns across different populations, including individuals with developmental language disorder, autism spectrum disorder, and other neurological conditions. By applying interpretable computational models, this work aims to identify reliable digital biomarkers for detection, classification, assessment, and longitudinal tracking.

Keywords: Digital Biomarkers, Neurological Disorders, Developmental Language Disorder, Autism, Explainable AI, Clinical Linguistics

Theme 3

Artificial Intelligence and Computational Language Analysis

Motivation: Artificial intelligence is increasingly shaping how language is produced, interpreted, evaluated, and used across social, educational, and technological contexts. This creates a need to better understand the capabilities, limitations, and biases of AI systems, as well as how they compare with human language use and communication.

Methodology: To meet this need, my research combines literature reviews, linguistic analysis, natural language processing, sentiment analysis, and computational approaches. I examine how AI systems process and generate language, how their outputs differ from or resemble human language, and how linguistic and social patterns can be used to evaluate AI performance, reliability, and bias.

Keywords: Artificial Intelligence, Natural Language Processing, Human–AI Writing, Sentiment Analysis, Algorithmic Bias, Computational Linguistics
